# CTFs & Practice Platforms

Hands-on practice platforms and wargames.

- **[TryHackMe](https://tryhackme.com/)** — gamified, beginner-friendly platform with structured guided paths across web security, networking, privilege escalation, and more.
- **[HTB Academy](https://academy.hackthebox.com/)** — structured, more rigorous technical pentesting modules; the next step up once TryHackMe's beginner content isn't enough.
- **[Hacker101](https://www.hacker101.com/)** — HackerOne's free web security/bug bounty training with its own CTF; distinct focus on bug bounty methodology.
- **[OverTheWire: Bandit](https://overthewire.org/wargames/bandit/)** — classic beginner SSH wargame teaching Linux/command-line basics through hands-on security puzzles.
- **[flaws.cloud](http://flaws.cloud/)** — classic deliberately-vulnerable AWS wargame (S3/IAM misconfigurations); the cloud-security equivalent of Bandit.
- **[AWSGoat](https://github.com/ine-labs/AWSGoat)** — self-deployed (via Terraform), covering more modern/complex AWS attack surface (Lambda, Cognito) than flaws.cloud's classic scenarios.
- **[IppSec](https://www.youtube.com/@ippsec)** — legendary channel of detailed Hack The Box walkthroughs, teaching pentesting methodology box by box.
- **[Cryptopals](https://cryptopals.com/)** — hands-on cryptography-breaking challenge set, same practice-platform format as Bandit, crypto-focused.
- **[picoCTF](https://picoctf.org/)** — Carnegie Mellon's extremely popular, beginner-friendly, always-available CTF practice archive and annual competition.
- **[Root-Me](https://www.root-me.org/?lang=en)** — large, long-standing platform with hundreds of challenges across web, crypto, forensics, network, and RE.
- **[Google CTF](https://capturetheflag.withgoogle.com/)** — Google's official CTF competition and challenge archive.
- **[CTFtime](https://ctftime.org/)** — the central hub/calendar for CTF competitions worldwide, with team rankings and writeup archives; distinct type (aggregator), not a practice platform itself.
